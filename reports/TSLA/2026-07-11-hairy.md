# Hairy's bear case: Tesla (TSLA) — 2026-07-11

## The case

Let's start where it hurts most: John's numbers. Automotive revenue — 69.5% of the company — fell 9.2% year-over-year to $65.8B from $72.5B. That alone is a story. But cross-reference it against the industry data I pulled: global EV sales rose 21% in 2025 to 21.2 million units. Tesla did not merely fail to grow in a shrinking market — it shrank by 9.2% while the category it invented grew by 21%. That's not "macro headwinds," that's a company losing share, hand over fist, in its own core business, in a market that's still expanding. If Tesla were merely riding a soft macro tide, its revenue would be flat-to-down-slightly, in line with a maturing category. Instead it fell by nearly a tenth while the tide rose by a fifth. The gap between those two numbers — roughly 30 percentage points — is the cleanest single data point in this entire file, and it belongs to the bears.

Now look at where the growth in EVs is actually coming from, because it matters for whether Tesla can claw the share back. U.S. EV sales — Tesla's home turf, the market it dominates most and where its brand should carry the most weight — grew from 1.52M to 1.53M units. That's +0.7%. Call it what it is: flat. The "macro tide" Baldy leans on is a China and Europe and India story (China +17.7%, Europe +34.8%, India +84.7%), not a U.S. story. Tesla's most defensible market is not growing, and Tesla's own automotive revenue is shrinking on top of that flat base. Combined with David's data point — U.S. consumer sentiment down 14.2% YoY to 44.80 and a rising 1.45 inventory/sales ratio — the demand-side story domestically is deteriorating, not accelerating.

Then there's the valuation, which is where this stops being a "tough year" and becomes a genuine mispricing. 371x trailing P/E and 158x forward P/E against a 4.77% ROE and a 4% operating margin is not a growth premium — it's a religious premium. Compare it to what the balance sheet is actually producing: $5.25B of free cash flow on $94.8B of revenue, an ROE that undershoots a savings account in a decent-rate environment, and margins compressing (gross margin 19.07%, down from healthier historical levels). A stock priced at 371x trailing earnings needs those earnings to compound at a blistering pace for years to "grow into" that multiple. Nothing in this filing set — not FSD, not Optimus, not China — has cleared that bar yet. It's all narrative, discounted at a valuation that assumes the narrative is already true.

And here's the tell that makes me most confident: insiders sold into this. April 2026 saw 96.1 million shares sold with an MSPR reading of -99.91 — as negative as that metric gets. June 2026 brought another 17.5 million shares out the door. John notes the arc: insiders bought the November 2025 bottom, then sold hard as the stock recovered into 2026. That is not tax-planning noise dressed up as a red flag — that is a sustained, multi-month, high-volume liquidation pattern by the people with the best view of whether FSD, Optimus, and the China story are as good as the multiple implies. If insiders believed the re-rating case Baldy is building, this is exactly the moment they'd be buying, not distributing.

Layer on the regulatory-credit cliff: -28% YoY to $1.99B from $2.76B. Those credits are pure-margin revenue with no manufacturing cost attached — they've been propping up Tesla's reported profitability for years. As they erode, the 4% operating margin is going to look worse before it looks better, because the credit subsidy is not a repeatable growth engine, it's a shrinking one. Combine that with 19% gross margin (compressed from prior periods) and you have a company where the *quality* of the earnings, not just the size, is deteriorating.

Finally, on engineering quality — Baldy is right that most of the ten NHTSA recall campaigns in the last three years are washer hoses, horn wires, and certification labels. I won't manufacture alarm where there isn't one; that's fair criticism of overreach elsewhere in this business. But I'd push back on "no systemic concern": one of those ten campaigns is a battery-pack contactor that "may fail," filed under Electrical System / Propulsion System — that's not a label or a seat fastener, that's the component that connects and disconnects high-voltage battery power. It's a safety-critical powertrain part, and it sits alongside 739 NHTSA consumer complaints logged for 2025-2026 model years alone. That's a real, if modest, quality signal worth tracking quarter over quarter — especially for a company simultaneously tearing down an established production line (Model S/X) in 46 days to stand up Optimus. Fast capital reallocation cuts both ways: it's either disciplined execution or the first sign that manufacturing discipline is being stretched thin while the company chases the next story.

Put it together: shrinking core revenue in a growing category, a valuation that requires flawless execution on unproven bets, insiders selling in size, a profitability crutch (credits) being kicked away, and a flat home market. This is a "story stock" priced as a certainty. I don't need Optimus to fail outright to be bearish — I just need the market to spend two quarters re-pricing automotive as an automotive business (which is what it is, by revenue) rather than a robotics call option, and 40-70% of this multiple is air.

## Rebuttal (round 2)

It's the market rewarding management for changing the subject. Runway is real (I don't dispute the $28.8B net cash), but runway measures how long you can afford to be wrong, not evidence that you're right. Ferragu's own framing — "two more quarters" to validate FSD — is a skeptic's clock, and the market is paying 371x trailing earnings *today* for a bet that isn't due to be graded until the very earnings calls Baldy cites as the risk to his own thesis.

Two problems. If Tesla is "gaining share" in a market that grew 17.7%, but *global* automotive revenue still fell 9.2%, then whatever Tesla is winning in China is being lost faster somewhere else — most plausibly the flat U.S. market and a competitive Europe. A share gain you can't quantify, inside a global revenue decline you can, is not a bull case; it's an unverified offset to a verified problem.

A 7:1 bullish tilt sitting on top of a 371x P/E and insider selling of this magnitude looks less like "conviction without euphoria" and more like a crowd that hasn't yet updated on the automotive-revenue and insider-selling data sitting right next to it in these same reports. Sentiment lags fundamentals; it doesn't validate them.
